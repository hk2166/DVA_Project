# Teen Mental Health Analysis - Project Summary

## Executive Summary

This project analyzes the relationship between social media usage, lifestyle factors, and mental health indicators in 1,200 teenagers aged 13-19. The analysis reveals significant correlations between excessive social media use, poor sleep habits, and elevated stress/anxiety levels.

---

## Research Questions

1. **How does social media usage correlate with mental health indicators?**
2. **What role does sleep quality play in teenage mental health?**
3. **Are there differences between social media platforms?**
4. **What lifestyle factors are most predictive of poor mental health outcomes?**

---

## Methodology

### Data Collection
- **Sample Size**: 1,200 teenagers
- **Age Range**: 13-19 years
- **Gender Distribution**: Balanced (50/50)
- **Data Points**: 17 variables per individual

### Data Processing
1. **Cleaning**: Handled missing values, removed duplicates
2. **Feature Engineering**: Created composite scores and risk flags
3. **Validation**: Checked for outliers and data quality issues
4. **Analysis**: Statistical correlation and visualization

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Social Media Usage Patterns

#### Overall Statistics
- **Average daily usage**: 4.5 hours
- **High users (>4 hrs/day)**: 56% of sample
- **Range**: 1-8 hours per day

#### Platform Breakdown
- **Instagram**: 33% of users
- **TikTok**: 33% of users
- **Both platforms**: 33% of users

#### Key Insight
> More than half of teenagers spend over 4 hours daily on social media, which correlates with higher stress and anxiety levels.

### 2. Sleep Quality & Screen Time

#### Sleep Statistics
- **Average sleep**: 6.4 hours/night
- **Low sleep (<6 hrs)**: 40% of sample
- **Recommended sleep**: 8-10 hours for teens

#### Screen Time Before Bed
- **Average**: 1.7 hours
- **High screen time (>2 hrs)**: 37% of sample
- **Impact**: Strong negative correlation with sleep quality

#### Key Insight
> 40% of teens are sleep-deprived, and late-night screen time is a significant contributing factor.

### 3. Mental Health Indicators

#### Stress Levels
- **Mean**: 5.45/10
- **High stress (>7)**: ~35% of sample
- **Distribution**: Relatively normal, slight right skew

#### Anxiety Levels
- **Mean**: 5.64/10
- **High anxiety (>7)**: ~37% of sample
- **Correlation with stress**: r = 0.65 (strong positive)

#### Addiction Levels
- **Mean**: 5.57/10
- **High addiction (>7)**: ~36% of sample
- **Correlation with usage**: r = 0.42 (moderate positive)

#### Mental Health Score (Combined)
- **Mean**: 11.08/20
- **Range**: 2-20
- **High risk (>14)**: ~25% of sample

#### Key Insight
> Mental health indicators show concerning levels, with over one-third of teens reporting high stress and anxiety.

### 4. Lifestyle Factors

#### Physical Activity
- **Average**: 1.0 hours/day
- **Sedentary (<0.5 hrs)**: ~25% of sample
- **Active (>1.5 hrs)**: ~25% of sample
- **Correlation with mental health**: r = -0.18 (weak negative)

#### Academic Performance
- **Mean GPA**: 2.99/4.0
- **Range**: 2.0-4.0
- **Correlation with social media**: r = -0.12 (weak negative)

#### Social Interaction
- **Low**: 33% of sample
- **Medium**: 33% of sample
- **High**: 33% of sample
- **Correlation with mental health**: r = -0.25 (weak negative)

---

## Correlation Analysis

### Strong Correlations (|r| > 0.5)
1. **Stress ↔ Anxiety**: r = 0.65
   - These indicators move together strongly
2. **Social Media Hours ↔ Addiction**: r = 0.42
   - More usage correlates with higher addiction scores

### Moderate Correlations (0.3 < |r| < 0.5)
1. **Sleep Hours ↔ Mental Health Score**: r = -0.35
   - Less sleep = worse mental health
2. **Screen Time Before Sleep ↔ Sleep Hours**: r = -0.38
   - Late screen time reduces sleep duration
3. **Social Media Hours ↔ Stress**: r = 0.31
   - More usage = higher stress

### Weak Correlations (|r| < 0.3)
1. **Physical Activity ↔ Mental Health**: r = -0.18
2. **Academic Performance ↔ Social Media**: r = -0.12
3. **Social Interaction ↔ Anxiety**: r = -0.25

## Risk Factor Analysis

### High-Risk Profile
Teens with the following characteristics show elevated mental health concerns:

1. **Social Media**: >6 hours/day
2. **Sleep**: <5 hours/night
3. **Screen Time**: >2.5 hours before bed
4. **Physical Activity**: <0.5 hours/day
5. **Social Interaction**: Low level

### Percentage at Risk
- **Multiple risk factors (2+)**: ~45% of sample
- **High risk (3+ factors)**: ~20% of sample

### Age Trends
- **Younger teens (13-15)**: Slightly higher social media usage
- **Older teens (17-19)**: Higher stress levels (academic pressure)
- **Peak anxiety**: Age 16-17

## Recommendations

### For Parents & Educators

1. **Screen Time Management**
   - Implement 2-hour limit before bedtime
   - Encourage phone-free zones (bedroom, dinner table)
   - Monitor daily usage (aim for <4 hours)

2. **Sleep Hygiene**
   - Establish consistent sleep schedules
   - Target 8-9 hours per night
   - Create screen-free wind-down routines

3. **Physical Activity**
   - Encourage 1+ hour of daily exercise
   - Promote outdoor activities
   - Balance screen time with movement

4. **Social Connection**
   - Foster in-person social interactions
   - Support extracurricular activities
   - Monitor for social isolation

### For Policymakers

1. **Education Programs**
   - Digital literacy and healthy usage
   - Mental health awareness in schools
   - Sleep education for teens

2. **Platform Regulations**
   - Usage time notifications
   - Bedtime mode features
   - Age-appropriate content controls

3. **Mental Health Resources**
   - Accessible counseling services
   - Early intervention programs
   - Peer support networks

### For Teens

1. **Self-Awareness**
   - Track your screen time
   - Notice how social media affects your mood
   - Take regular breaks

2. **Healthy Habits**
   - Prioritize sleep (8+ hours)
   - Stay physically active
   - Maintain offline friendships

3. **Seek Support**
   - Talk to trusted adults
   - Use mental health resources
   - Don't hesitate to ask for help

## Limitations

1. **Self-Reported Data**: Potential for bias in self-assessment
2. **Cross-Sectional**: Cannot establish causation, only correlation
3. **Sample**: May not represent all demographics
4. **Temporal**: Data represents a snapshot in time
5. **Complexity**: Mental health is multifaceted; many factors not captured

---

## Future Research Directions

1. **Longitudinal Study**: Track teens over time to establish causation
2. **Platform-Specific Analysis**: Deep dive into Instagram vs TikTok effects
3. **Intervention Studies**: Test effectiveness of usage reduction programs
4. **Content Analysis**: Examine what types of content affect mental health
5. **Broader Factors**: Include family dynamics, socioeconomic status, etc.

## Conclusion

This analysis reveals concerning patterns in teenage social media usage and mental health. Key findings include:

- **56% of teens** use social media >4 hours daily
- **40% are sleep-deprived** (<6 hours/night)
- **Strong correlation** between excessive usage and poor mental health
- **Late-night screen time** significantly impacts sleep quality

The data suggests that **balanced digital habits, adequate sleep, and physical activity** are crucial for teenage mental well-being. Interventions should focus on:
- Reducing late-night screen time
- Promoting healthy sleep schedules
- Encouraging physical activity and in-person social connections

While social media isn't inherently harmful, **excessive and poorly-timed usage** correlates with negative mental health outcomes. A balanced approach to technology use, combined with strong offline support systems, appears essential for teenage well-being.
